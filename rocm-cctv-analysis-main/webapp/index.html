How an answer is built

Each video is analysed once, in the background, and the results are stored in a local SQLite database. Questions are answered from those stored facts. No language model writes the answers, so TRACE cannot invent a person or a moment.

  1. 01

    Sample

    ffmpeg reads the video at 5 frames per second.

  2. 02

    Detect

    YOLOX-Nano finds people, cars, trucks, motorcycles and bicycles.

  3. 03

    Track

    A tracker gives every object one ID for as long as it stays in view.

  4. 04

    Read pose

    RTMPose places 17 body joints on each person, so actions like a raised hand can be measured.

  5. 05

    Describe

    Rules derive colours, speed, path and events. CLIP indexes appearance for searches like “red jacket”.

  6. 06

    Answer

    Your question is matched to a search, answered from stored facts, and returned with timestamps.

Optional: with Ollama and the Moondream model installed, TRACE adds a one-line description of each person. It runs locally too.

What you can ask

Questions are matched to the measurements TRACE keeps. Clicking a timestamp in an answer moves the video to that moment and highlights the person or vehicle involved.

Questions about identity, age, gender or ethnicity are declined. The analysis does not measure them.

Limits worth knowing

The analysis is measurement, and measurements have edges. Check important answers against the video.

  • The small detector misses very distant or heavily hidden people.
  • Track numbers can swap when people cross closely, and hold only within one video.
  • Colours are approximate; lighting changes them.
  • Body actions need people at least about 60 px tall and are estimated from joint positions.
  • Night and infrared footage has not been tested.
